## Basic Rules

Here's a list of basic rules to abide by. 

1. You can only play one land per turn (unless an effect states otherwise).

Meaning to say that whenever it is your turn, only one land can be played. 

2. There is a stack and it dictates what the first effect will happen.

There is a data structure called a "stack" and the idea behind it is that First In, Last Out. So the last one in will be the first to resolve (meaning its effects will happen first before anything under it)

3. There are phases to each turn in magic.

The phases are as follows: 
- Untap
- Upkeep
- Draw
- First Main Phase
- Combat
- Second Main Phase
- End Step

Generally, in all of magic, these are the phases you will take sequentially EACH TURN unless stated otherwise (i,e., "there will be another combat step").

There are many other "subphases" like "beginning of combat", "end of combat", "on somebody's end step (so like its their end step declared and they cannot do anything else because they've declared the end of their turn)".

4. Cards have timing properties

I'll explain this further in the Cards section of this page.

5. You tap lands for mana

Although lands are NOT the only source of mana, lands are the very first source of mana that you will have and that you should use. There are many different kinds of lands, but unless you play with 2 colors and above, basic color lands will do you just fine.
## Phases

I'll explain here more in-depth what exactly happens in each phase.

### Untap Phase

In the untap phase, everything that has been tapped before will get to untap. So consider it a refresh. 

### Upkeep Phase

Nothing really happens in this step besides any triggered abilities during this phase or any cost that has to be paid during upkeep. But generally speaking, this is when a player gains priority for the first time during their turn.

### Draw Step

Very self-explanatory, you draw a card from your library. The amount of cards generally is 1 card only unless stated otherwise by any effect.

### First Main Phase

This is when you will get to play your cards! The land for turn, the creatures, the sorceries, the instants, enchantments, artifacts, auras, anything! (Provided that you have the mana for it).

### Combat Phase

This is when creatures can start fighting. Here, creatures that either have haste or do not have summoning sickness can start attacking another player. Additionally, instants can be played during this phase. Be warned, though, that you need mana during this step should you want to cast spells. Remaining mana from the main phase (meaning any mana that you are holding and can be used) will fizzle out. But should you have untapped sources (like lands) during combat, you can make mana again with that.  

In combat, there are steps the attacking player must follow. They must declare attackers first and must tap the creature they're attacking with and to whom it is directed towards (unless the creature has vigilance, which means it does not need to tap). Afterwards, the defending player can now declare blockers with their creatures should they have any.

Then once everything is declared, damage can now resolve.

### Second Main Phase

You can play anything once again here, provided you have the mana for it. You can play ANOTHER land for turn IF STATED THAT YOU CAN. 

### End Step

This is where cleanup happens. The end step has no turn-based actions. Once it begins, the active player gets priority. If the active player’s hand contains more cards than their maximum hand size (normally seven), they discard enough cards to reduce their hand size to that number. Additionally all damage marked on permanents (including phased-out permanents) is removed and all “until end of turn” and “this turn” effects end. This turn-based action doesn’t use the stack.

### Timings

Every single card, besides lands of any kind, are what we would call **spells**. 
A creature card is a spell, an enchantment is a spell, an instant or sorcery is a spell. But these spells have different timing restrictions. 

It is best to separate these spells as **instants** or **sorceries**. Although these are card types in of themselves, they explain clearly timing rules. 

**Instants** are instant, they can be casted ANYTIME at ANY MOMENT.
**Sorceries** on the other hand can only be casted during main phases (unless stated otherwise with the use of the keyword "Flash")

The only instant card types are instant spells and those spells with the keyword "Flash". Everything else, like the creatures, artifacts, enchantments, auras, equipment, are all sorceries.

Additionally, creatures are affected by this thing called **Summoning Sickness**. 
Meaning they cannot use their ACTIVATED (tapped) abilities NOR CAN THEY ATTACK ON THE TURN YOU SUMMONED THEM unless they have the **keyword haste**.
